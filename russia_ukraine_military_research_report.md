# Latest Military Activity: Russia-Ukraine War

## Executive Summary

The Russia-Ukraine War continues with intense military activity across multiple fronts, featuring significant Ukrainian counteroffensives, Russian strategic attacks, escalating drone warfare, and increasing NATO involvement. The period from April to September 2025 has seen both diplomatic initiatives and continued fierce fighting, with both sides seeking military advantages while peace negotiations remain stalled.

## Current Military Situation

### Ukrainian Advances and Counteroffensives

**Northeastern Front Progress:**
- Ukrainian forces claimed significant advances in the Sumy region, driving Russian forces back 1-2.5 kilometers along critical front lines
- Ukrainian military reports active combat operations to "destroy the enemy and liberate our settlements"
- Advances achieved despite Ukraine being outgunned and outmanned in certain sectors

**Strategic Drone Campaigns:**
- Successful Ukrainian strikes on Primorsk, Russia's largest oil-loading port on the Baltic Sea
- SBU drones struck three critical oil pumping stations feeding the Ust-Luga port terminal
- Oil hub suspension potentially costing Russia up to $41 million daily in lost revenue
- Ukraine developed new weapon system launching anti-aircraft missiles from remote-controlled speedboats

### Russian Offensive Operations

**Continued Ground Operations:**
- Russian forces maintaining pressure in northeastern regions, particularly around Sumy
- Kremlin seeking territorial gains amid diplomatic efforts to end the war
- Heavy artillery and assault operations concentrated near eastern towns like Pokrovsk

**Aerial Campaigns:**
- Large-scale drone attacks with Russia claiming ability to launch mass attacks of 100,000+ drones
- September 12 attacks involved 221 Ukrainian drones allegedly intercepted by Russian air defenses
- Continued missile strikes on Ukrainian cities and civilian infrastructure
- Russia employing Iranian-designed Shahed drones and domestic Geran variants

### NATO Escalation and Airspace Violations

**Unprecedented Incursions:**
- Poland experienced largest Russian drone incursion with 19 drones entering NATO airspace
- At least 3 Russian drones shot down by Polish forces in single incident
- Lithuania, Estonia, and Latvia preparing for increased drone threats
- Baltic states clarifying legislation to enable defensive actions against hostile drones

**NATO Response:**
- Lithuania declared readiness to shoot down drones violating NATO airspace
- NATO countries updating rules of engagement for drone threats
- Joint defense cooperation between Poland and Ukraine on drone and anti-drone systems
- NATO experts working with Baltic states on air defense improvements

## Diplomatic Developments

### Peace Negotiations

**Istanbul Talks:**
- Three rounds of Ukraine-Russia peace talks held in Istanbul between May and July 2025
- Both sides achieved prisoner exchanges and repatriation of soldier remains
- Russia presented peace proposal demanding Ukraine cede territory, rejected by Kyiv
- Kremlin declared talks on "pause" as of September 2025

**International Diplomatic Activity:**
- Trump-Putin summit in Alaska under "Pursuing Peace" banners
- Zelensky's crucial White House meeting with President Trump
- European leaders forming "coalition of the willing" for Ukraine peace efforts
- UK providing $193 million in additional aid to Ukraine

### Failed Ceasefire Attempts

**Easter Truce Violations:**
- One-day Easter ceasefire (April 2025) systematically violated by both sides
- Ukraine reported nearly 3,000 Russian ceasefire violations
- Russia claimed over 1,000 Ukrainian violations, including 900+ drone attacks
- Heavy fighting continued despite diplomatic commitments to pause

## Military Technology Evolution

### Drone Warfare Advancements

**Cost Asymmetry:**
- Russian drones cost $10,000-$100,000 vs. $4 million Patriot interceptors
- Ukraine developing indigenous drone capabilities with increasing sophistication
- Both sides investing heavily in unmanned systems for strategic advantage

**Electronic Warfare Impact:**
- Electronic warfare measures causing drones to deviate from planned routes
- Occasional drone entries into NATO airspace due to EW interference
- Both sides developing countermeasures and improved navigation systems

## Infrastructure Targeting

**Energy Infrastructure:**
- Ukraine successfully targeting Russian oil export infrastructure
- Russia continuing strikes on Ukrainian energy facilities
- Both sides denying civilian targeting while striking dual-use infrastructure

**Strategic Economic Impact:**
- Ukrainian strikes on Primorsk potentially disrupting $15 billion in annual Russian oil revenue
- Infrastructure attacks becoming increasingly strategic and economically focused

## Internal Developments

**Ukrainian Anti-Corruption Efforts:**
- Arrest of several politicians in defense sector corruption scheme
- $42 million UK aid package for power grid repairs and energy infrastructure protection
- Ukraine maintaining governance reforms despite ongoing conflict

## Regional Implications

**Baltic Security:**
- NATO eastern border facing unprecedented security challenges
- Baltic states implementing enhanced air defense measures
- Joint military exercises and cooperation increasing among NATO allies

**NATO-Russia Tensions:**
- Moscow denying intentional targeting of NATO countries
- Putin claiming it's "impossible to believe" Russia would attack NATO
- Despite denials, drone incursions creating heightened tensions

## Conclusions and Outlook

The Russia-Ukraine War continues to evolve with increasing sophistication in military technology, particularly drone warfare. While diplomatic efforts have intensified, including direct US involvement, military operations persist with no clear resolution imminent. The conflict shows increasing potential for spillover effects on NATO territory, particularly through drone incursions and airspace violations.

Both sides are adapting their military strategies, with Ukraine focusing on strategic infrastructure targeting and asymmetric warfare capabilities, while Russia maintains numerical advantages and continues investment in unmanned systems. The coming months will likely see continued intense fighting alongside diplomatic efforts, with the risk of escalation remaining high.

- Ukrainian advances: 1-2.5 kilometers in Sumy region
- Russian drone capacity: Potential for 100,000+ drone mass attacks
- Economic impact: $41 million daily potential loss from Primorsk suspension
